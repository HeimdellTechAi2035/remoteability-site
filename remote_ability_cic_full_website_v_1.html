About RemoteAbility CIC

A practical route into remote, flexible and meaningful work.

RemoteAbility CIC was created in response to the UK disability employment gap. Too many disabled people, those with mental health conditions, and neurodivergent individuals want to work but are blocked by inaccessible roles, benefit uncertainty, and a lack of training that fits real lives. Our model is built from lived experience and designed to offer something different: a supported path into modern, remote-first jobs.

The challenge we’re tackling

Disabled people are far less likely to be in work than non-disabled people. Many roles still expect commuting, fixed hours and environments that simply don’t work for people managing pain, fatigue or fluctuating conditions. At the same time, the benefits system can make even trying work feel risky.

  • ✓ Remote and flexible roles are still not the norm, even where they could be.
  • ✓ Accessible, supportive digital skills training is still hard to find.
  • ✓ Confidence, structure and community are often missing for people out of work long term.

How our model works

We take people from “I’m not sure where to start” to “I have real experience, income and a path forward” using a four-phase system that blends accessibility, training and real work.

Phase 1
Set Up & Outreach

We build the infrastructure, partnerships and referral routes that connect disabled jobseekers to real opportunities.

Phase 2
Equip & Enable

We help participants secure equipment, software and support through Access to Work and other schemes.

Phase 3
Train & Deploy

We provide live, supported experience on real campaigns using real systems and clients.

Phase 4
Scale & Progression

We support people into ongoing roles, freelance work or extended contracts, with mentoring and wellbeing check-ins.

Programmes & support

From first conversation to long‑term remote careers.

Our programmes are built around a simple idea: give people the tools, support and experience they need to succeed in real jobs, not just training rooms. Each phase has a clear purpose and outcome.

Phase 1 – Set Up & Outreach

We start by building the ecosystem that makes everything else possible: accessible tools, clear pathways and trusted partnerships.

  • ✓ Accessible CRMs, VoIP platforms and cloud storage configured for remote teams.
  • ✓ Inclusive training materials designed for different learning styles and access needs.
  • ✓ Referral routes with councils, charities, Jobcentres and training providers.

Phase 2 – Equip & Enable

Before training begins, we help each person secure the equipment, software and adjustments they need – often funded through Access to Work.

  • ✓ Support to apply for Access to Work and similar schemes.
  • ✓ Identifying the right mix of devices, software and ergonomic support.
  • ✓ “Remote readiness” checks to ensure people can work safely and comfortably from home.

Phase 3 – Train & Deploy

Participants take part in live, supported work – not just mock exercises. They build confidence, skills and evidence by contributing to real campaigns.

  • ✓ Training in communication, outreach, digital tools and ethical sales.
  • ✓ Hands‑on use of CRMs, call systems and messaging platforms.
  • ✓ Opportunities to earn early through supervised, commission‑linked work.

Phase 4 – Scale & Progression

The goal is long‑term employment and progression, not just a short‑term placement.

  • ✓ Progression into freelance, part‑time or full‑time roles with partner organisations.
  • ✓ Advanced modules in areas such as compliance, data handling and AI‑enabled services.
  • ✓ Ongoing mentoring, peer support and wellbeing check‑ins.

Access to Work support

Removing the “I can’t afford to start” barrier.

Many people want to work but can’t afford the equipment, software or support they need to get started. We help participants understand, apply for and use the UK government’s Access to Work scheme so these costs don’t block progress.

What Access to Work can help with

Access to Work is a government grant that can pay for practical support so disabled people can start or stay in work. For many RemoteAbility participants, it’s the key that makes remote employment possible.

• Laptops and devices suitable for remote work

• Ergonomic desks, chairs and workstation equipment

• Assistive software such as screen readers or speech‑to‑text

• Specialist training for using access technology

• Job coaching or support workers

• Help with additional travel or communication needs

How we support you

1. Check eligibility
We talk through your situation and help you understand whether Access to Work is likely to be available to you.

2. Identify what you need
Together we look at equipment, software, adjustments and support that would make remote work safe and sustainable for you.

3. Complete the application
We support you to complete your application in a clear, accurate way and to communicate with advisers if needed.

4. Set up and settle in
Once support is agreed, we help you set up your equipment, understand how to use it and integrate it into your training or role.

We don’t charge participants for help with Access to Work. Our role is to make the process less confusing, not more expensive.
