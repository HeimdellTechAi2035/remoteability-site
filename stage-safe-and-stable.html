Stage One: Safe and Stable

Weeks 1–2 of the Six-Week Pathway to Independence Programme. Full assessment, urgent barriers identified, referrals and support plans put in place.

🛟

Main Objective

By the end of Week 2, every participant should have:

  • ✓A named support worker or mentor
  • ✓A personal progression plan
  • ✓Identified urgent needs
  • ✓Appropriate referrals in progress
  • ✓A clear employment, self-employment or business pathway
  • ✓Agreed short-term and long-term goals
1️⃣

Week 1: Understanding the Person and Their Immediate Needs

What We Do

💬 Session 1: Welcome and Initial Conversation

Informal, respectful and non-judgemental. Participants are given time to explain:

  • Their current situation
  • What they want to change
  • What support they already receive
  • What has prevented them from working
  • Whether they want employment or self-employment
  • What worries them most
  • What they believe they are good at

We never assume somebody is ready for work simply because they have enrolled.

📋 Session 2: Full Needs Assessment

Each participant completes a supported assessment covering:

🏠 Housing

  • Sleeping rough
  • Sofa surfing
  • Temporary accommodation
  • At risk of eviction
  • Supported accommodation
  • Stable housing
  • Housing applications already submitted
  • Local authority contact
  • Support worker involvement

🌱 Health & Recovery

  • Physical health
  • Mental health
  • Medication
  • Addiction or dependency
  • Existing treatment
  • Rehabilitation needs
  • Recovery support
  • Accessibility requirements
  • Immediate risk or safeguarding concerns

💷 Financial Position

  • Benefits
  • Universal Credit
  • Personal Independence Payment
  • Debt
  • Rent arrears
  • Bank account
  • Access to money
  • Budgeting support
  • Benefits advice required

🪪 Documentation

  • National Insurance number
  • Identification
  • Birth certificate
  • Passport
  • Proof of address
  • Bank details
  • Right-to-work documentation
  • Email address
  • Telephone number

💻 Digital Access

  • Smartphone
  • Laptop or computer
  • Internet access
  • Email skills
  • Online form skills
  • Password management
  • Basic computer confidence
  • Assistive technology requirements

💼 Work & Business History

  • Previous employment
  • Qualifications
  • Volunteering
  • Informal work
  • Skills
  • Hobbies
  • Business ideas
  • Customer service experience
  • Sales experience
  • Previous barriers at work

⚡ Session 3: Immediate Action Plan

Urgent issues are prioritised. This may include:

  • Contacting the council homelessness team
  • Beginning a housing application
  • Referring to emergency accommodation
  • Connecting with a rehabilitation service
  • Referring to mental-health support
  • Arranging benefits advice
  • Obtaining identification
  • Opening a bank account
  • Accessing food, clothing or hygiene support
  • Applying for a phone, device or internet access
  • Connecting with safeguarding services

We Don't Just Provide Telephone Numbers

Where practical and appropriate, staff help the participant:

  • ✓Make the call
  • ✓Complete the form
  • ✓Prepare evidence
  • ✓Attend or prepare for appointments
  • ✓Understand letters
  • ✓Record the outcome
  • ✓Arrange follow-up action

Week 1 Outputs

✓ Completed needs assessment
✓ Risk and safeguarding assessment
✓ Immediate needs plan
✓ Referral record
✓ Named mentor
✓ Accessibility plan
✓ Initial personal goals
✓ Agreed contact arrangements
2️⃣

Week 2: Stabilisation and Choosing the Right Pathway

🔁 Session 1: Reviewing Urgent Support

The mentor reviews progress on:

  • Housing referrals
  • Rehabilitation referrals
  • Health support
  • Benefits
  • Documentation
  • Banking
  • Communication access
  • Equipment
  • Safeguarding concerns

Any failed referral is followed up rather than treated as completed.

🌟 Session 2: Skills and Strengths Discovery

Participants identify their:

  • Practical skills
  • Digital skills
  • Communication skills
  • Life experience
  • Personal strengths
  • Interests
  • Previous responsibilities
  • Informal experience
  • Community knowledge
  • Creative abilities
  • Problem-solving skills

The purpose is to show participants that employable and commercial skills are not limited to formal qualifications.

Session 3: Choosing a Progression Route

Every participant chooses one primary pathway:

Pathway A: Employment

Suitable for people who want part-time, full-time, remote, flexible, or supported employment, or a gradual return to work.

Pathway B: Freelancing or Self-Employment

Selling a skill or service independently — e.g. virtual assistance, content creation, cleaning, gardening, repairs, administration, design, photography, tutoring, delivery or local services.

Pathway C: Starting a Business

For participants with a viable idea, reasonable stability, capacity to deliver, a defined customer, willingness to find clients, and an understanding that income isn't guaranteed.

Pathway D: Preparation and Recovery

Recovery, volunteering, routine-building, confidence work, digital skills, supported activity, housing stabilisation, health treatment, continued mentoring. This is still progress — not failure.

Week 2 Outputs

✓ Personal strengths profile
✓ Chosen progression pathway
✓ Six-week goal
✓ Ninety-day goal
✓ Support and adjustment plan
✓ Referral follow-up schedule
✓ Personal progression plan
